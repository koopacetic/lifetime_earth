# Front-of-book diagram: ideas

> **Tabled (2026-11-20)** until the end of the project. The idea may end up as text in the introduction instead.

Purpose: one opening spread that gives the whole 4.57 billion years at a glance before the reader starts turning
5 Myr pages. Sketches (black and grey, drawn from `data/timescale.json`):
[sketches/front-diagram-ideas.png](sketches/front-diagram-ideas.png). Regenerate with `./run sketches`.

| | Idea | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **24-hour clock**: all of time = one day; humans arrive in the last ~6 s | Instantly intuitive; a classic | Short eons become slivers; circular reading |
| B | **Spiral**: time winds outward; the present is the outer tip | Beautiful; fits a square page; long and short both visible | Harder to read exact proportions |
| C | **Powers-of-ten zoom**: each bar = the last tenth of the bar above | Solves "where are humans?"; previews the final-page zoom insets | Abstract; needs explanation |
| D | **This book's foredge, to scale**: the book block with its eon bands | Ties the diagram to the physical object; the reader can check it on the closed book | Only works once foredge bands are final |
| E | **One calendar year**: 1 day ≈ 12.5 Myr; dinosaurs gone Dec 26; humans in the last ~35 min | Very relatable | Familiar (Sagan's Cosmic Calendar) |
| F | **One ribbon with callouts**: a single proportional strip, handful of milestones | Simple and honest; good as a fold-out or endpaper | Recent history is invisible |

A suggestion: an opening spread with **D** (left page: "this book") and **C** (right page: "and the last page, zoomed").
Together they explain the book's two big ideas: physical thickness = time, and the crowded last page.
Any milestone shown in these diagrams must also be cited.
